    Methods and data

    What is measured

    A renter household is stretched when rent and utilities take 30% or more of its income. The share comes from the Census American Community Survey one-year estimates, table B25070: households in the 30%-or-more categories divided by all renter households whose ratio can be computed. Rent is median gross rent; income is median renter household income; both are adjusted to 2023 dollars with the annual CPI-U.

    Jobs and pay are counted at workplaces in the county (BLS QCEW, all covered jobs, through 2023). Commuting shares come from Census LODES primary jobs, through 2019.

    Which counties

    The ten most populous counties in each of Colorado, Massachusetts and Washington in 2011, fixed before any results were seen. 390 county-years, 2011–2024 without 2020.

    Survey error

    Every survey figure has a 90% margin. A change is called real only when it is larger than the margin of the change. The margins use the Census formula for a proportion.

    How the forecasts were tested

    Each forecast stands on December 31 and predicts the next year using only figures published by then: survey results one year late, workplace jobs two, commuting four. 2023 and 2024 were held back as the final test. The three-year average and partial pooling were added after that test had been scored and are labelled as added later.

    What this does not show

    Thirty large counties in three states do not stand for the United States. The relationships here are associations, not causes. Publication delays are assumed, and today's revised files stand in for what was available at the time. Median renter income describes whoever rents that year, so it mixes raises with changes in who rents.
